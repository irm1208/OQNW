# UK/EU Peptide Legality Audit

**Prepared:** 10 July 2026
**Scope:** All compounds currently in the OmniQure catalog (including hidden/unlisted "Weight Management" SKUs: Retatrutide, Cagrilintide, Tirzepatide, and reference-only Semaglutide). Research/report only — no code or data changes were made as part of this task.

**Disclaimer:** This is a compliance risk summary for internal decision-making, not legal advice. Regulatory status of borderline/research-chemical peptides shifts quickly and varies by exact marketing claims and buyer jurisdiction. Confirm with qualified legal counsel (UK and, ideally, per-EU-member-state) before relying on any conclusion here for a live storefront serving UK/EU customers.

---

## 1. Regulatory framework

**United Kingdom** — Human Medicines Regulations 2012 (HMR 2012) governs what counts as a "medicinal product." The MHRA decides borderline cases based on presentation and function: a substance is treated as a medicine if it is marketed *as if* intended to treat, prevent, or diagnose disease, or to restore/modify a physiological function — regardless of a "not for human consumption / research use only" disclaimer, if the overall marketing (dosing charts, health claims, before/after imagery, testimonials) implies human use. Products found to be unlicensed medicinal products are illegal to sell in the UK; several specific hormone classes (see §3) are additionally scheduled as Prescription Only Medicines (POM).

**European Union** — Directive 2001/83/EC (as amended by the Falsified Medicines Directive 2011/62/EU) applies the same "medicinal product by function or presentation" test at EU level, but licensing/enforcement is delegated to each member state's national medicines agency (e.g. BfArM in Germany, ANSM in France). There is no EU-wide harmonized "research peptide" exemption; a product legal to list in one member state's interpretation can still be prosecutable in another. Treat the EU as 27 separate risk profiles, not one.

**Common thread for both jurisdictions:** the "research use only, not for human consumption" disclaimer is a marketing artifact, not a legal shield. Regulators and courts look at the *substance of the marketing* (site copy, blog content, dosing guides, testimonials, chat-bot answers, FAQ, protocol pages) to decide whether a product is being presented as a human medicine. This matters directly for OmniQure, since the storefront currently carries extensive consumer-facing dosing/protocol content, testimonials, and a chat assistant that answers questions about "research use."

## 2. Highest-risk compounds

These are GLP-1/incretin-class and melanocortin compounds with an unambiguous, high-profile enforcement history as unlicensed medicines when sold direct to consumers for weight loss or cosmetic use.

| Compound (catalog SKUs) | Why it's highest risk |
|---|---|
| **Tirzepatide** (10/15/30/45/60 mg) | Licensed prescription-only medicine in both the UK (Mounjaro, POM) and EU. MHRA and press (Sept 2025 MHRA alert, reported by national media through 2026) have specifically warned about illegal, non-prescription tirzepatide/semaglutide-class "weight loss jabs" sold online and via social media, including product not manufactured to any verified standard. Selling this compound outside a licensed pharmacy/prescriber channel is medicine-supply-without-authorisation in the UK and equivalent offences EU-wide. |
| **Retatrutide** (10–60 mg) | Not yet an approved medicine anywhere (still in Eli Lilly Phase 3 trials per NIHR briefing, July 2025). Because it has no licensed use, any human-directed sale is automatically supply of an unlicensed medicinal product if marketed with health/weight-loss claims — currently one of the most-cited compounds in UK/EU media warnings about illegal "research chemical" GLP-1 knockoffs (e.g. Independent/TikTok coverage, 2025–2026). |
| **Cagrilintide** | Same profile as Retatrutide: unapproved amylin analogue, sold in the "research chemical" grey market almost exclusively for human weight-loss use; same enforcement exposure. |
| **Semaglutide** (reference copy only — no live SKU) | Licensed POM in UK (Ozempic/Wegovy) and EU. Even though OmniQure has no purchasable Semaglutide product, any remaining marketing copy that discusses dosing/benefits for this named compound sits in the same enforcement spotlight as the above three and should stay fully removed from public copy, consistent with the other three. |
| **Melanotan I / Melanotan II** | Long-standing MHRA/Trading Standards target (BBC investigations 2013 "tanning jabs," 2022 "dangerous tanning products," ongoing nasal-spray warnings). Never licensed in the UK or EU; sold almost exclusively for cosmetic tanning use, which is squarely "presentation as a medicinal product." Among the most consistently enforced peptide categories in the UK. |

**Recommendation:** these five names carry materially higher legal exposure than the rest of the catalog and should be the first candidates kept off (or removed from) public UK/EU-facing storefronts, or gated behind an age/professional-use verification and stripped of all consumer health-benefit framing, dosing-for-humans language, and testimonials.

## 3. Elevated-risk compounds (recognised hormone/peptide drug classes)

| Compound | Note |
|---|---|
| **Gonadorelin**, **Kisspeptin-10** | Gonadorelin is a defined GnRH analogue under UK law (The Medicines (Gonadotrophin-Releasing Hormone Analogues) (Restrictions on Private Sales and Supplies) Order 2024), a class the UK government tightened private-sale rules on in 2024. Kisspeptin acts on the same axis and is being developed clinically as a reproductive-hormone drug. Both read as recognisable hormone therapies rather than inert "research chemicals," raising presentation risk. |
| **Sermorelin, Tesamorelin, CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Hexarelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6** | Growth-hormone secretagogues. Tesamorelin is a licensed POM in the US (Egrifta) though not UK/EU-approved; the whole class is frequently sold to consumers for bodybuilding/anti-aging with explicit dosing protocols, which is the classic "presentation as medicine" trigger even though none are UK/EU-licensed drugs. |
| **IGF-1 LR3** | Potent growth-factor analogue; commonly flagged in anti-doping and bodybuilding contexts; no licensed medical use — human-directed marketing puts it in the unlicensed-medicine category. |
| **Oxytocin** | Oxytocin itself is a licensed POM (used in labour induction); a peptide product marketed with human dosing protocols for off-label uses is presenting an unlicensed use of a controlled drug substance. |
| **Thymosin Alpha-1** | Licensed as a medicine in some non-UK/EU markets (marketed as Zadaxin elsewhere) but not approved in the UK/EU; consumer marketing for immune support edges toward medicinal presentation. |

## 4. Lower-risk / genuinely niche research compounds

The remaining catalog — **BPC-157, TB-500 (and Fragment 17-23), AOD-9604, DSIP, Epithalon / N-Acetyl Epithalon Amidate, GHK-Cu, GLOW/KLOW blends, KPV, LL-37, MOTS-C, NAD+, P21, PE-22-28, Selank / N-Acetyl Selank Amidate, Semax / N-Acetyl Semax Amidate, SS-31, VIP, 5-Amino-1Q, ARA-290, and combination blends** — are, per current UK MHRA guidance and market commentary (peptide.law, Peptide Authority, etc.), generally described as **unscheduled** under the Misuse of Drugs Act and not subject to a specific licensing regime by name. They are not "legal to sell for human use" — they remain unlicensed medicinal products the moment marketing presents them as treating or modifying a physiological function in a person — but they carry materially lower public-enforcement profile and are the ones most commonly sold today by comparable UK-based peptide retailers under a research-use framing.

This is the category where the "research use only, not for human consumption, no health claims" discipline (no dosing-for-humans language, no testimonials implying self-administration, no dosing charts as advice) is most load-bearing, because it is the entire basis for these products currently being tolerated in the market rather than actively enforced against.

## 5. Cross-cutting compliance gaps observed on the storefront (not fixed as part of this task)

These are noted for awareness only, per this task's read-only scope:

- **Consumer-facing content undermines the research-use framing.** Protocol pages, storage guides, FAQ, glossary and chat-bot content across the site describe dosing frequency, cycle lengths, and benefits in ways that read as guidance for personal/human use rather than laboratory research — this is exactly the "presentation" test regulators use, independent of the disclaimer text.
- **"Human/vet/personal use" chat-bot answer is good practice** (already states research-only, no human/veterinary use) but is inconsistent with the rest of the site's dosing-protocol content, which weakens its effect.
- **Jurisdiction warning banner** currently only flags Australia (TGA Schedule 4); it does not mention UK HMR 2012 medicinal-product status or per-EU-member-state risk, despite UK/EU apparently being primary markets (site copy, £ pricing, DHL "UK orders 1–2 days").

## 6. Non-binding recommendations

1. Treat Tirzepatide, Retatrutide, Cagrilintide, Semaglutide, and Melanotan I/II as the priority review set — highest enforcement precedent in UK/EU media and MHRA alerts.
2. For the remaining catalog, keep strictly to inert, laboratory-framed copy (no dosing-for-a-person language, no testimonials, no "how it feels" content) — this is the main lever currently keeping these products in lower-risk territory.
3. Consider extending the jurisdiction warning banner (or adding a UK/EU-specific one) alongside the existing Australia TGA banner, given UK/EU appear to be primary served markets.
4. Get UK solicitor and at minimum German/French counsel sign-off before any renewed marketing of the GLP-1/incretin-class compounds, given the specific 2025–2026 MHRA and national-press enforcement attention on that category.
